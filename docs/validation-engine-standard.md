# Verilogix Validation Engine Standard

**Version:** 1.0
**Status:** Foundational

---

# Purpose

The Validation Engine is the authority responsible for determining whether a project, stage, activity, deliverable, assessment, or output satisfies governance requirements.

The Validation Engine does not perform work.

The Validation Engine evaluates work.

The Validation Engine determines compliance.

---

# Principle

Completion does not equal compliance.

Evidence does not equal validation.

Approval does not replace validation.

Validation exists to determine whether progression has been earned.

---

# Constitutional Authority

The Validation Engine derives authority from:

1. Constitution
2. Governance Model
3. Framework Rules
4. Stage Registry
5. Project Registry

Validation decisions shall remain subordinate to constitutional principles.

---

# Validation Object

Every validation shall contain:

* Validation ID
* Validation Type
* Validation Scope
* Validation Rule
* Validation Result
* Validator
* Timestamp
* Supporting Evidence

No validation may exist without traceability.

---

# Validation Types

Verilogix supports multiple validation types.

---

## Document Validation

Determines whether required documents exist and satisfy requirements.

Examples:

* Contract Review
* Permit Review
* Technical Drawing Review
* Assessment Review

Outputs:

```text
PASS
FAIL
CONDITIONAL PASS
```

---

## Evidence Validation

Determines whether evidence satisfies governance requirements.

Examples:

* Photographic Evidence
* Site Inspection Evidence
* Audit Records
* Customer Documentation

Outputs:

```text
VALID
INVALID
INSUFFICIENT
```

---

## Technical Validation

Determines whether technical requirements have been satisfied.

Examples:

* Engineering Package Review
* CAD Validation
* Production Specification Review
* Equipment Compliance Check

Outputs:

```text
PASS
FAIL
```

---

## Operational Validation

Determines readiness for execution.

Examples:

* Expedition Readiness
* Resource Availability
* Staff Allocation
* Supplier Confirmation

Outputs:

```text
READY
NOT READY
```

---

## Risk Validation

Determines whether risk is acceptable.

Examples:

* Weather Risk
* Safety Risk
* Commercial Risk
* Resource Risk

Outputs:

```text
ACCEPTABLE
UNACCEPTABLE
ESCALATE
```

---

## Commercial Validation

Determines commercial readiness.

Examples:

* Signed Agreement
* Deposit Received
* Payment Confirmed
* Budget Approved

Outputs:

```text
PASS
FAIL
```

---

## AI-Assisted Validation

Artificial Intelligence may assist validation.

AI may:

* Review documents
* Compare requirements
* Identify gaps
* Recommend outcomes

AI may not become the sole authority for progression.

Human accountability remains mandatory.

---

# Validation Scope

Validation may occur against:

* Project
* Stage
* Activity
* Deliverable
* Document
* Assessment
* Approval Request

The scope must always be declared.

---

# Validation Rules

Every validation shall be governed by rules.

Rules must be:

* Explicit
* Objective
* Repeatable
* Auditable

Examples:

```text
Permit must exist.
Deposit must be received.
Risk score must be below threshold.
Technical review must be completed.
```

Undefined rules are prohibited.

---

# Validation Results

Every validation shall produce a result.

Valid outcomes:

```text
PASS
FAIL
CONDITIONAL PASS
INSUFFICIENT EVIDENCE
ESCALATE
```

Results must be retained.

Results must be auditable.

---

# Conditional Pass

A conditional pass allows progression only when defined conditions are satisfied.

Example:

```text
Permit approved subject to insurance confirmation.
```

Conditions must be documented.

Conditions must be tracked.

---

# Validation Failure

Validation failure shall prevent progression.

Failure may result from:

* Missing evidence
* Missing dependency
* Rule violation
* Unacceptable risk
* Incomplete documentation

Failed validation must generate findings.

---

# Findings

A finding represents a governance issue identified during validation.

Finding categories:

```text
Minor
Major
Critical
```

Critical findings shall block progression.

---

# Escalation

Certain validation outcomes require escalation.

Examples:

* High risk
* Regulatory issue
* Contractual conflict
* Safety concern

Escalation authority must be defined within the framework.

---

## Human Validator

---

## Automated Validator

Examples:

* Workflow Validator

## AI Validator

Examples:

* Document Review Agent
* Compliance Analysis Agent

---

# Validation Lifecycle

Every validation follows the same lifecycle.

```text
Requested
      ↓
In Review
      ↓
Evidence Checked
      ↓
Rule Evaluation
      ↓
Decision Issued
      ↓
Recorded
```

Alternative paths:

```text
Rejected
Escalated
Returned
```

---

# Validation Audit Requirements

Every validation shall record:

* Validation ID
* Validator
* Validation Type
* Rule Set Used
* Evidence Reviewed
* Decision
* Timestamp
* Findings
* Escalations

Validation activity must be fully auditable.

---

# Governance Effect

Validation determines progression authority.

Without validation:

* Approval is invalid.
* Progression is invalid.
* Governance is incomplete.

Validation is the bridge between execution and progression.

---

# Foundational Principle

Work is not considered complete because it was performed.

Work is considered complete only when it has been validated against defined governance requirements.
