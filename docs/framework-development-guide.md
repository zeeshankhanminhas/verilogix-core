# Verilogix Framework Development Guide

**Version:** 1.0
**Status:** Foundational

---

# Purpose

This guide defines the standard process for creating new frameworks within Verilogix.

The purpose of framework development is to transform domain expertise into governed execution.

Frameworks allow Verilogix to operate across multiple industries while maintaining a common constitutional foundation.

---

# Constitutional Requirement

All frameworks shall comply with:

* Constitution
* Governance Model
* Project Registry Standard
* Stage Registry Standard
* Dependency Engine Standard
* Evidence Registry Standard
* Validation Engine Standard
* Approval Engine Standard
* Intelligence Engine Standard

Framework development begins with governance.

Not software.

---

# Framework Development Lifecycle

Every framework shall be developed using the following sequence:

```text
Domain Definition
        ↓
Outcome Definition
        ↓
Stage Design
        ↓
Dependency Mapping
        ↓
Evidence Design
        ↓
Validation Design
        ↓
Approval Design
        ↓
Intelligence Design
        ↓
Framework Review
        ↓
Framework Approval
        ↓
Framework Release
```

---

# Phase 1 — Domain Definition

Define the operational domain.

Questions:

* What industry is being governed?
* What activities occur?
* What outcomes are delivered?
* What risks exist?
* What regulations apply?

Examples:

```text
Expedition Operations
Engineering Services
Manufacturing
Security Operations
Training Services
Compliance Audits
```

Output:

```text
Domain Definition Document
```

---

# Phase 2 — Outcome Definition

Define the outcomes the framework must govern.

Examples:

RF:

```text
Safe Expedition Delivered
```

MIDTS:

```text
Approved Engineering Package Delivered
```

BOXIT:

```text
Manufactured Product Delivered
```

Outcome-first design is mandatory.

---

# Phase 3 — Stage Design

Identify the progression journey.

Questions:

* What stages exist?
* What sequence occurs?
* Where are decisions made?
* Where are risks introduced?

Example:

```text
Lead
    ↓
Qualification
    ↓
Planning
    ↓
Validation
    ↓
Approval
    ↓
Execution
    ↓
Review
```

Output:

```text
Framework Stage Registry
```

---

# Phase 4 — Dependency Mapping

Identify prerequisites.

Questions:

* What must exist?
* What must be completed?
* What must be approved?
* What resources are required?

Examples:

```text
Passport
Permit
Contract
Payment
Qualification
Equipment
```

Output:

```text
Dependency Register
```

---

# Phase 5 — Evidence Design

Determine proof requirements.

Questions:

* What evidence is required?
* What proves completion?
* What proves compliance?

Examples:

```text
Assessment Forms
Photographs
Contracts
Reports
Certificates
Customer Sign-Off
```

Output:

```text
Evidence Register
```

---

# Phase 6 — Validation Design

Determine evaluation requirements.

Questions:

* What must be reviewed?
* What constitutes compliance?
* What rules apply?

Examples:

```text
Technical Validation
Risk Validation
Commercial Validation
Operational Validation
```

Output:

```text
Validation Register
```

---

# Phase 7 — Approval Design

Determine authority structure.

Questions:

* Who may approve?
* Who may reject?
* Who may escalate?

Examples:

```text
Operations Manager
Engineering Lead
Commercial Director
Framework Owner
```

Output:

```text
Approval Matrix
```

---

# Phase 8 — Intelligence Design

Determine learning mechanisms.

Questions:

* What should be learned?
* What should be measured?
* What should improve?

Examples:

```text
Customer Feedback
Supplier Performance
Operational Lessons
Audit Findings
```

Output:

```text
Intelligence Register
```

---

# Phase 9 — Framework Review

Review the framework against constitutional standards.

Verification checklist:

* Stages defined
* Dependencies defined
* Evidence defined
* Validation defined
* Approval defined
* Intelligence defined

Framework gaps shall be resolved before approval.

---

# Phase 10 — Framework Approval

Framework approval shall be performed by:

```text
Framework Owner
```

Approval confirms:

* Constitutional compliance
* Governance completeness
* Operational readiness

---

# Phase 11 — Framework Release

Approved frameworks become active.

Framework status:

```text
Draft
Review
Approved
Active
```

Only active frameworks may govern projects.

# Governance Outcome

A completed framework should answer:

* What work is being governed?
* What progression looks like?
* What dependencies exist?
* What evidence is required?
* What validation is required?
* Who approves progression?
* How learning is captured?

If these questions cannot be answered, the framework is incomplete.

---

# Foundational Principle

Frameworks are not process maps.

Frameworks are not workflows.

Frameworks are governance systems that transform domain expertise into repeatable, validated, auditable execution.
